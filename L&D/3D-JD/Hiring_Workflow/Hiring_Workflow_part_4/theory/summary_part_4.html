Overall purpose
The document defines the must-have technical baseline for an AI-first assessment platform,
focusing on implementation capabilities rather than detailed policy or escalation mechanics.
Key areas covered
• Assessment & Question Bank: Supports MCQs, coding/IDE, SQL, project-based tasks,
system design, subjective, matching/ordering and file-upload questions. Question-bank
processing is AI-led, with human SME/governance review triggered only by defined risks
such as ambiguity, duplicates, broken solutions, difficulty mismatch or compliance concerns.
• Assessment Integrity: Defines Full Integrity Mode for candidates and Browser Integrity
Mode for candidates and internal learners. Violations are divided into Zero-Tolerance and
weighted Standard Violations, with different applicability and weights depending on the
assessment scenario. Copy/paste itself is intended to be blocked rather than scored.
• Integrity Index: Uses one unified trust measure: High trust, Medium trust or Low trust.
Standard violations generate a weighted raw score, capped at 100 for normalization. By
default, normalized scores of 0–29 = High trust, 30–59 = Medium trust, and 60–100 =
Low trust. Any qualifying Zero-Tolerance violation overrides the score and forces Low trust.
• Identity & Plagiarism: Continuous AI-based face matching validates candidate identity.
Coding plagiarism is evaluated within the same assessment cohort using both similarity
outliers and a configurable ceiling, with a default ceiling of 90%. Plagiarism remains separate
from the Integrity Index.
• AI Evaluation Rubrics: Evaluation dimensions have fixed Platform Admin-configured
weights. AI can choose which permitted dimensions apply to a question, but cannot
dynamically change their weights. Coding and SQL have proficiency-level-specific
weighting, including special treatment for entry-level PL1 assessments.
• AI Question Quality: AI-generated questions are quality-scored across six areas including
structural validity, completeness, correctness, bias/fairness, clarity and originality. Pass
thresholds are 0.80 for MCQ, 0.75 for Coding and 0.80 for SQL/Query.
• Reporting: Defines fixed fields for Web/PDF and Excel reports, covering candidate details,
completion/status information, Integrity Index, plagiarism, disconnections and assessment
scores. Web/PDF additionally includes detailed question information, webcam/screen
evidence and a chronological activity log.
• Communication Assessment: Defines AI-based evaluation for Listening, Speaking,
Reading and Writing, using 1–5 rubric scores normalized to 0–100. Pass thresholds are
Listening 60, Speaking 65, Reading 55 and Writing 60.
• Mother Tongue Influence (MTI): MTI is generally advisory, but for Customer Service roles it
becomes a hard gate, requiring MTI ≤35%.
• Hire/No-Hire logic: All required hard gates must pass. Failure of any single hard gate
results in No Hire. Overall CEFR is derived from the four LSRW areas: Listening, Speaking,
Reading and Writing.
In one line
The document provides the technical blueprint for an AI-first assessment platform covering
question generation, automated evaluation, proctoring/integrity, plagiarism, communication
scoring, reporting and automated trust/hiring decision inputs, while limiting human
intervention mainly to risk-triggered question-bank review.
